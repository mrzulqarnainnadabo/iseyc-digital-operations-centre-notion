# Design-reference review

The five references were inspected read-only on 8 November 2026. They informed general layout and browsing patterns only; no page layout, imagery, or copy was copied.

[SceneAI][1] uses a dark promotional landing page with a centered hero, bright CTA, filter controls and dense image-led gallery. The useful ideas are a clear opening hierarchy and scannable categories. Its promotional tone, vivid gradient and dense previews are poor fits for a nonpartisan institution.

[Landbook][2] pairs a compact header and search with visible topic categories, filters and a card gallery. Its topic grouping and scannable metadata are useful patterns, but the commercial framing, ads and emphasis on inspiration imagery are not.

[60fps][3] uses a spacious light landing page, a centered message and labeled topic shortcuts above app previews. The design supports clear orientation and predictable browsing; its consumer-gallery framing and motion-heavy previews should not be adopted for public evidence pages.

[Recent Design][4] makes a broad collection browsable with topic filters, sorting and concise card attribution. That is useful for organizing a larger evidence catalogue in the future. Its dense masonry feed, sponsored content and competing side rail would distract from current public-service information.

[Landdding][5] combines a compact two-level navigation, search and a large hero with categorized preview cards. The most relevant takeaways are clear separation of primary and secondary pathways and concise resource metadata, not its commercial gallery, voting or sign-in controls.

## Applied direction

IDCC uses the existing ISEYC green, warm white and restrained gold with editorial headings, explanatory summaries, structured department priorities, explicit provenance panels and a shared evidence classification checklist. It avoids gallery imagery, advertisements, unsupported counters, political visuals and fabricated maps. Small-screen navigation is a keyboard-operable native disclosure menu with full-size links; the public experience respects reduced-motion preferences and retains visible focus styles.
