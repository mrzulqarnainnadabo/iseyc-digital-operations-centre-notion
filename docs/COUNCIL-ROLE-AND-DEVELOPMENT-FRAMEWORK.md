# ISEYC Presidential Council — Role and Development Framework

Status: proposed role architecture for institutional review. It does not appoint any person, amend the constitution or grant system permissions.

## Design objective

Build a deliberately small, productive Presidential Council. Each office must have:
- one clear institutional purpose;
- a bounded set of responsibilities and outputs;
- a reporting and coordination line;
- a repeatable weekly operating rhythm;
- a personal development pathway;
- explicit limits on decision and financial authority.

The role guide in the Operations Centre is a learning and operating aid. The authoritative appointment record remains the existing **IDCC — Appointment & Role Register**. Its current records are templates, not appointments.

## Recommended lean structure

### Presidential leadership
- National President & Chair of the Presidential Council.

### Executive coordination
- Chief of Staff — coordinates the President's priorities and council follow-through.
- Secretary-General — institutional records, minutes, correspondence and procedure.
- Chief Operations Officer — cross-department execution and delivery coordination.
- Chief Financial Officer — budgets, financial records, controls and reporting.

### Department leadership
- Head of Education and Capacity Building.
- Head of the Department of Health and Well-being.
- Head of Software and Technology.
- Head of the Civic Department.

### Institution-wide leadership
- Youth Leadership Coordinator.
- Director of Public Engagement & Awareness.
- Director of Member Welfare & Social Development.

### Independent governance
- Chair, Disciplinary Committee. This office must operate under the approved constitution and fair-hearing procedure, with conflict-of-interest controls.

### Advisory
- Special Advisers to the National President. These are time-bounded, subject-specific advisory assignments, not a parallel command structure.

This gives a compact core with clearly defined specialist responsibilities. Special advisers should be appointed only when a real expertise gap or defined assignment exists; they should not automatically become standing council members.

## Department naming recommendation

Use these names consistently in the Operations Centre and public institutional materials:
1. **Education and Capacity Building**
2. **Department of Health and Well-being**
3. **Software and Technology**
4. **Civic Department**

The Civic Department covers civic education, community concerns, institutional responsibilities and accountability. Software and Technology covers software capability, digital inclusion, institutional systems and responsible technology.

Existing Notion display labels such as “Software & Technology” and “Civic Department” must be mapped to stable department identifiers. Do not silently rename or use display text as a security key.

## How the council should operate

- The President sets institutional direction and chairs council deliberations.
- The Chief of Staff coordinates priorities and follows up on cross-institutional commitments.
- The Secretary-General maintains the authoritative record of meetings, resolutions and correspondence.
- The COO coordinates execution and dependencies across departments.
- The CFO maintains financial control and prepares financial information for authorised decisions.
- Department heads own their programme pipeline, evidence, work plans, reports and requests for approval.
- Institution-wide directors coordinate their defined functions across departments.
- The Disciplinary Committee handles only formally referred matters and must protect procedural fairness.
- Advisers provide bounded advice; they do not command departments.

## Department operating cycle

Every department should use the same simple cycle:
1. **Review** — examine available evidence, source quality, date, geography and limitations.
2. **Propose** — identify a practical need, intended beneficiaries, objective and expected result.
3. **Prepare** — create a programme brief, work plan, resource estimate, risks and evidence plan.
4. **Submit for approval** — route the proposal to the correct authority. The system must show who can approve it; a generic shared staff key is not an approver identity.
5. **Deliver** — begin only after the required approval and resource checks.
6. **Document** — record activities, outputs, evidence and issues without inventing impact.
7. **Review and learn** — assess results and recommend the next step.

Automation should handle reminders, completeness checks, routing, document preparation and status visibility. It must not independently approve programmes, commit funds, make public claims or mark outcomes verified.

## Role-based personal development and email cadence

Future role-based communications should be useful and respectful, not motivational spam:
- On confirmed appointment: welcome note, role purpose, authority boundaries, first-30-day guide and support contact.
- Weekly: one role-specific leadership lesson, one practical action and one learning resource.
- Monthly: reflection prompt, development check-in and recognition of documented contribution.
- Quarterly: role review, learning needs and appointment/access review reminders.

Before email automation is activated, require a verified individual identity, confirmed appointment, valid official contact address, consent or applicable lawful basis, frequency controls, unsubscribe handling, delivery logs and a safe way to correct the role/contact record. Never send role-sensitive material to an unverified or shared inbox.

## Governance and safety

- Role templates do not appoint people.
- Appointment records do not by themselves grant application permissions.
- Access requires verified personal identity, approved active membership, an active appointment, explicit permissions and exact resource scope.
- Financial approval, disciplinary outcomes, membership decisions, publication and official dispatch must remain separate authorised actions.
- Shared-key access is transitional and cannot identify who performed an action.
- No placeholder names, invented appointees, assumed attendance, fake performance data or automated approval.

## Implementation sequence

1. Approve the role architecture and reconcile department identifiers.
2. Connect the existing Appointment & Role Register read-only and display its real template/appointment states.
3. Build a member-facing role guide and personal onboarding pathway.
4. Implement identity, membership and appointment checks before enabling individual officer workspaces.
5. Add role-specific notification preferences and email automation.
6. Connect department workflows to the shared review/approval process.
7. Conduct real mobile and desktop workflow testing before national presentation.
