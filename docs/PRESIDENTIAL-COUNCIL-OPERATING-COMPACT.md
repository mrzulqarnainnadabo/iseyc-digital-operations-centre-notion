# ISEYC Presidential Council — Operating Compact

Status: proposed operating standard for review. This document does not amend the constitution, appoint officers or create financial, disciplinary, publication or system authority.

## 1. Purpose

The Presidential Council is a small coordination and oversight body. It exists to convert ISEYC's approved institutional priorities into clear departmental work, preserve governance, resolve cross-department dependencies and review evidence of progress.

The Council should not become a second management layer for every routine task. Department heads manage their approved work within their documented limits. The Council focuses on direction, decisions reserved to it, institutional risk and matters that cross departmental boundaries.

## 2. Operating principles

- One accountable owner for every action; supporting contributors are named separately.
- Every proposal states the need, evidence, intended beneficiaries, expected output, resources, risks and approval required.
- Preparation is not approval. A draft, AI-generated recommendation or meeting note is not an institutional decision.
- No spending, public commitment, appointment, formal dispatch or restricted activity proceeds without the appropriate recorded authorisation.
- The Secretary-General maintains the authoritative record; the system is a working aid and must not silently replace approved records.
- Report exceptions and decisions needed; do not create activity merely to fill a dashboard.

## 3. Core responsibilities

| Office | Primary operating responsibility | Must not do alone |
|---|---|---|
| National President / Council Chair | Set direction, chair council deliberations, resolve matters reserved to the President and ensure accountability | Bypass governing documents or treat a system status as formal authority |
| Chief of Staff | Coordinate presidential priorities, prepare decision briefs and follow up cross-institutional commitments | Approve departmental work or spending by virtue of coordination |
| Secretary-General | Maintain records, procedure, minutes, resolutions and formal correspondence workflow | Certify draft minutes or issue formal correspondence without sign-off |
| Chief Operations Officer | Coordinate approved delivery, dependencies, work plans and operational review | Change policy, approve reserved decisions or commit funds |
| Chief Financial Officer | Prepare budgets, maintain financial records, report financial position and flag control risks | Unilaterally approve and execute a payment |
| Department Heads | Own evidence review, programme preparation, delivery within approval and reporting | Launch unapproved programmes or publish unsupported claims |
| Institution-wide Directors | Coordinate their defined cross-department function and provide guidance | Create parallel command over department heads |
| Disciplinary Committee Chair | Coordinate fair, confidential handling of formally referred matters | Decide a case with a conflict or outside approved procedure |
| Special Advisers | Provide time-bounded subject expertise and written advice | Direct staff or departments unless separately authorised |

## 4. Council operating rhythm

### Weekly executive coordination
A short exception-led review led by the Chief of Staff and COO:
1. What approved work moved?
2. What is blocked, and who owns the next action?
3. What decision or resource is required?
4. What evidence supports the status?
5. What must be escalated to the President or Council?

This is a coordination meeting, not a substitute for a formal Council sitting.

### Monthly Presidential Council
The Secretary-General prepares the agenda and prior decision/action record. Department heads submit concise updates using the same structure:
- work completed with evidence;
- work in progress and next milestone;
- blockers and support required;
- proposals requiring approval;
- risks, incidents or safeguarding concerns;
- learning and capacity needs.

The President chairs. The Secretary-General records draft minutes and proposed resolutions. The Council distinguishes discussion, recommendation and formal decision.

### Quarterly institutional review
Review whether the council remains appropriately small, whether each role has a real workload and output, whether controls are functioning, and whether appointments or access need review. Remove redundant reporting rather than adding meetings.

## 5. Decision and approval gates

| Matter | Preparation / recommendation | Required control before action |
|---|---|---|
| Department programme | Department Head, supported by COO | Approval by the authority defined in ISEYC's governing and delegated-authority documents |
| Budget or expenditure | CFO checks budget, evidence and controls | Separate authorised approval; payment execution must be controlled and recorded |
| Public statement or publication | Responsible department and Public Engagement Director | Named authorised reviewer / signatory and source check |
| Appointment or role change | President / designated appointment process with Secretary-General record | Confirm constitutional process, identity and appointment; access granted separately |
| Formal council resolution | Council deliberation; Secretary-General drafts record | Valid meeting procedure, quorum and approval under governing documents |
| Disciplinary matter | Formal referral and committee process | Conflict check, notice, fair hearing, documented outcome and any required appeal |
| System access or sensitive data | System administrator recommends scoped access | Verified individual identity, active appointment, least-privilege approval and audit logging |

If ISEYC's constitution or approved policy does not clearly assign an authority, the system must show **“Authority not configured — refer for governance review”**. It must not guess.

## 6. Department approval cycle

1. Review available data and record source, date, geography, quality and limitations.
2. Define the need and intended beneficiaries.
3. Prepare a concise concept note, work plan, resource estimate, risks and evidence plan.
4. Submit to the configured approval authority.
5. Proceed only after the decision and any budget or safeguarding checks are recorded.
6. Document delivery and outputs with supporting evidence.
7. Review results and recommend the next step.

Automation may check completeness, prepare drafts, route submissions, remind owners and show status. Automation must not approve, sign, publish, disburse funds or verify impact.

## 7. Meeting record standard

Every formal Council meeting should have:
- meeting type, date, time, venue or link;
- chair and meeting owner;
- agenda and pre-reading;
- required participants and recorded attendance/apologies;
- decisions sought;
- draft minutes and proposed resolutions;
- action owner, due date and linked decision;
- review and approval status.

A shared staff key cannot prove who attended, who voted or who approved. Do not claim quorum, verified attendance or approved minutes until the relevant process and identity controls exist.

## 8. Lean council test

Before creating or retaining an office, ask:
1. Is this responsibility essential and recurring?
2. Is it already owned by another office?
3. Does the office produce a clear output?
4. Can the work be automated or shared without weakening accountability?
5. Is there enough real workload to justify a standing council seat?

Special advisers should normally be assignment-based and time-limited. Communications should remain a shared institutional function coordinated by the Public Engagement & Awareness Director, not automatically a fifth department.

## 9. Review and adoption

The National President and appropriate governing body should review this compact against ISEYC's constitution, existing policies and actual delegation of authority before adoption. The system should expose unresolved authority as a visible gap rather than invent a rule.
