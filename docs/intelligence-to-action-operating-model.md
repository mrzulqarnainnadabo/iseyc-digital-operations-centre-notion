# ISEYC Intelligence-to-Action Operating Model

**Status:** Working institutional architecture  
**Scope:** Four departments, one shared evidence foundation, one internal operations record  
**Product-facing evidence name:** Intelligence Observatory

## 1. Institutional design

ISEYC operates one institution-wide intelligence-to-action process. The four departments are the institution's delivery and interpretation units. They are not separate data silos and they do not compete to own the evidence foundation.

The **Intelligence Observatory** is shared infrastructure available to all four departments. It maintains source provenance, dated observations, geographic scope, measurement definitions, evidence classifications and known limitations. It is not a fifth department and it is not limited to the Civic Department's subject area.

The **Civic Department** is the institutional steward for Civic Mandate and Civic Brain. Stewardship does not restrict access: all departments may use Civic Brain for evidence interpretation within their own mandates.

## 2. Product responsibilities

| Component | Institutional responsibility | Must not be represented as |
|---|---|---|
| Intelligence Observatory | Source registry, evidence gateway, indicator and snapshot provenance, geographic and temporal context, limitations, and evidence-quality state | A live feed when a record is a dated snapshot; a complete national or local census |
| Civic Mandate | Intake and human review of community submissions; authoritative public record of items explicitly approved for publication | A prevalence survey, a poll, an automatic statement of community consensus, or proof of government action |
| Civic Brain | Evidence interpretation, structured briefs, source-linked findings, uncertainty and evidence-gap explanation for all departments | An authority that approves programmes, verifies implementation by itself, contacts institutions, or resolves public issues |
| Digital Operations Centre (DOC) | Internal programme, work-plan, decision, action, meeting, report and outcome records; accountable ownership and institutional follow-through | A public evidence source or a substitute for verified identity, appointment or delegated authority |

The Observatory supplies evidence. Mandate supplies reviewed public community records. Brain helps interpret both. DOC records what ISEYC decides and does, who is accountable, what was delivered, and what evidence supports a reported result.

## 3. The four departments

### 01 — Education and Capacity Building
Uses evidence to identify learning-access and participation questions, school and learning-environment conditions, teacher capacity, literacy, practical skills and lifelong-learning needs. It turns supported findings into scoped learning or capacity programmes with defined participants, geography, deliverables and assessment methods.

### 02 — Department of Health and Well-being
Uses evidence to examine primary healthcare access, facility conditions, essential medicines and service availability, maternal and child health, prevention and community well-being. It distinguishes population indicators, administrative service records, facility observations and individual community reports. It does not make clinical claims or infer prevalence from isolated reports.

### 03 — Software and Technology
Uses evidence to understand digital inclusion, connectivity, digital literacy, software capability, innovation ecosystems, digital public infrastructure and responsible technology. It also supports the reliability, privacy, security, accessibility and interoperability of ISEYC's own systems. It does not convert programme reach or hub-directory entries into national digital-literacy or technology-adoption rates.

### 04 — Civic Department
Stewards Civic Mandate and Civic Brain, supports non-partisan civic learning and helps interpret public responsibilities, institutional mandates, community concerns and accountability evidence. It distinguishes a public body's legal or administrative responsibility from proof that a service was delivered. It does not endorse parties, candidates or campaigns or claim that published submissions represent the whole population.

## 4. Shared department workflow

Every department follows the same controlled sequence:

1. **Frame the question.** State the public problem, intended population, geography, period and decision the department needs to make.
2. **Find evidence.** Search the Observatory and relevant published Mandate records. Use Brain to help interpret—not replace—source review.
3. **Check fitness.** Confirm publisher, source URL, reference period, retrieval date, geography, units, measurement type, method, limitations and evidence status.
4. **Record the assessment.** Save the evidence references, what is supported, what is inferred, what is unknown, and the confidence or quality limitation.
5. **Propose action.** Create a programme or work-plan proposal with accountable role, intended outcome, target group, geography, deliverables, dates, dependencies and acceptance criteria.
6. **Authorise separately.** Approval, budget authority, external commitments and publication require the appropriate verified human authority. A proposed record is not approval.
7. **Deliver and document.** Record activities, source documents, service or participant evidence, risks, delays and changes. Do not treat activity counts as outcomes.
8. **Assess outcomes.** Compare the intended result with suitable follow-up evidence, state what changed and what cannot be attributed to ISEYC. Preserve negative, null and inconclusive findings.

A department may decide that evidence is insufficient and commission further collection rather than create a programme. An evidence gap is a valid operational result.

## 5. Minimum evidence record

Every observation used to justify a material programme or public claim should retain:

- Stable evidence and source identifiers.
- Publisher, original title and source URL.
- Reference period and retrieval/last-checked date as separate fields.
- Geography level and geography label; do not silently downscale national or state data to an LGA, ward, facility or household.
- Value, unit, denominator and measurement type where applicable.
- Source method, release/vintage and uncertainty interval where supplied.
- Evidence class and quality/review state.
- Limitations, missingness and known coverage gaps.
- Department relevance and the reason it is relevant.
- The DOC decision, programme or report that cites it.

Do not coerce text values, ranges, targets or campaign statuses into numeric indicators. Do not combine different denominators or reference periods without an explicit, documented method.

## 6. Evidence and claim boundaries

Use these distinctions consistently:

- **Official published data:** an attributable publisher release or dataset; not automatically complete, current or independently audited by ISEYC.
- **Administrative record:** records generated by a service or agency; coverage and reporting completeness must remain visible.
- **Survey or modelled estimate:** preserve population, sample/method, reference period, uncertainty and estimation status.
- **Programme announcement or target:** evidence that a commitment or target was announced, not that it was delivered.
- **Community submission:** an attributed observation or request; not a prevalence estimate or representative sample.
- **Implementation evidence:** dated evidence that an activity or service occurred; not automatically proof of quality or impact.
- **Independently verified outcome:** only use when the verification method, scope, date and verifier are documented.

AI-generated analysis is an interpretation aid. It must cite actual evidence IDs, distinguish source facts from inference, disclose missing evidence and uncertainty, and remain subject to human review. AI must not invent statistics, auto-publish, approve a programme, verify an outcome or claim an issue is resolved.

## 7. Geography and pilot discipline

The pilot must keep three distinct scopes visible:

- Nigeria-wide evidence: national context only.
- Kaduna State evidence: state context only.
- Kaduna South/LGA/ward/facility evidence: only when a source actually measures that geography.

A Kaduna State value is not a Kaduna South value. A national rate is not a state rate. A selected directory entry is not proof of local operating capacity. Where local evidence is missing, display the gap and define the next responsible collection step; never substitute a national or state proxy without an explicit, defensible method.

## 8. Department value: what evidence should change

| Department | Evidence should help the department | Example of a measurable result (not a pre-filled target) |
|---|---|---|
| Education and Capacity Building | Choose where and what learning support is justified; identify who is excluded; adapt programme content | Demonstrated change in a defined skill or learning measure, with baseline, follow-up and participant denominator |
| Department of Health and Well-being | Prioritise health education or service-access work; identify facility/service questions requiring verification | Documented service-access or knowledge change using an appropriate, ethically collected measure |
| Software and Technology | Choose relevant digital skills, access or software interventions; assess infrastructure and ecosystem constraints | Demonstrated competency, successful service use, accessibility or reliability change—not registrations alone |
| Civic Department | Improve civic understanding, trace public responsibilities and follow up on published community concerns | Better source-backed understanding, completed accountable follow-ups or verified institutional responses—not submission volume alone |

These are measurement examples, not claims that baselines, programmes or outcomes already exist.

## 9. Internal record model

DOC remains the system of record for ISEYC's internal work. A department programme or work item should reference:

- Department and accountable role.
- Problem statement and target population/geography.
- Evidence IDs and the assessment that justified the work.
- Intended outcome, baseline status and measurement method.
- Activities, deliverables, dates, dependencies and risk.
- Approval status and the authority/decision record where approval exists.
- Implementation evidence and report references.
- Outcome assessment, limitations and next decision.

A report generated from work registers is a draft register snapshot until a human adds the relevant evidence and submits it. A status of Completed records the work-register status; it does not certify impact.

## 10. Governance and release gates

- Keep public and private records separated. Only explicitly published Mandate records may enter public-facing community views.
- Preserve the current stable department IDs and routes. Display names may change without renaming historical Notion options or breaking integrations.
- Map both legacy Notion select labels and canonical department labels to the same stable IDs. Unknown values remain Unspecified and require review.
- Shared-key staff access is not verified individual identity, appointment or delegated authority. Do not infer role-based permissions from it.
- Keep approval, submission, publication, external dispatch and verification as distinct human-controlled actions.
- Do not deploy or merge this operating-model change without the required release approval.
- Do not describe the system as nationally complete until source coverage, geographic coverage, operational workflows and verification have been demonstrated.

## 11. Implementation order

1. Canonical department names and stable-ID/legacy-select mapping.
2. Evidence contract and quality-state validation across Observatory and Brain.
3. Department-specific evidence views using the same shared contract.
4. Brain analysis context for all four departments, with evidence-ID citations and clear fact/inference separation.
5. DOC programme and work-plan records linked to evidence assessments and approval decisions.
6. Implementation evidence and outcome review, with explicit gaps and limitations.

**Operating principle:** one shared evidence foundation; four distinct departmental mandates; one accountable institutional record of decisions and results.
